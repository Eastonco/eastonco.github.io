## Frontend Engineering

My roots are in frontend work: React, TypeScript, and GraphQL have been the core of most of what I've shipped, from Expedia Group's customer-facing checkout flows to the Next.js site this CV lives on. I've also worked at the architecture level here — building a modular monorepo using Qiankun and NX that let 25+ teams at Expedia ship independent micro frontends into one unified platform.

## Backend & Systems

I've grown into a strongly full-stack engineer, working across Node.js, Kotlin, Spring Boot, Postgres, and Redis in my day-to-day at Expedia. Python shows up heavily in my side projects — the Free Stuff Finder's scraper and classifier pipeline, for instance — and I'm comfortable moving between JVM and JS/TS backends depending on what a project calls for.

## Infrastructure & DevOps

Most of my side projects share the same infrastructure pattern: a Raspberry Pi at home, Docker for containerization, and a Cloudflare Tunnel to expose the service to the public internet — the receipt printer and Free Stuff Finder both run this way. On the professional side, I've implemented Okta authentication at scale (cutting onboarding time 90% at Expedia) and worked with Vercel/Supabase for lighter-weight, fully-hosted projects like checkrides.fyi.

## AI & LLM Engineering

This is the thread that ties almost everything together. At Expedia, I lead adoption of MCP and Claude Code across the Experience Manager platform, ran a live Claude Desktop training for 800+ employees, and am now working with principal engineers and SVPs on a company-wide skill governance plan. Outside of work, the same instincts show up constantly: checkrides.fyi and the receipt printer were both vibe-coded with Claude, and the Free Stuff Finder uses Claude Haiku as an intent-based classifier rather than relying on brittle keyword matching. I'm as interested in the infrastructure side of AI adoption (governance, tooling, MCP servers) as I am in using AI to build things quickly.

## How It All Connects

The common thread across my professional and side work isn't any single language or framework — it's using AI as a force multiplier to take an idea from concept to working proof-of-concept fast, whether that's a governance framework used by hundreds of engineers or a receipt printer that lets anyone in the world print to my desk for fun.
