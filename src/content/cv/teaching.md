# Teaching & Mentorship Experience

## Teaching Assistant, CS 122 – Data Structures & Algorithms
**Washington State University | Senior Year**

My teaching journey started during my senior year of college as a TA for CS 122, the second course in the computer science major and most students' first real introduction to data structures — linked lists, hash tables, and all the foundational building blocks that everything else in the major builds on.

I led a lab section of 15–20 students, meeting Monday, Wednesday, and Friday at 7:30 AM. I'm not a morning person, so showing up energized to a room full of half-awake college students and getting real work done in 90 minutes was its own kind of challenge — but it was also a blast.

This is where I discovered how much I genuinely loved teaching. It was something I'd wanted to do throughout college but wasn't sure I'd be able to fit in around credit requirements and graduation timelines. I made it work, and it turned out to be one of the most rewarding parts of my undergrad experience. I went beyond the standard TA role and was the only TA in the course to organize class-wide review sessions ahead of midterms and finals, running 200–300 students through a full recap of the semester's material. I don't track hard metrics from those sessions, but a handful of students I taught went on to work at Fortune 500 companies — a nice reminder of the ripple effect that kind of teaching can have, even if I've since lost touch with them.

## Microsoft TEALS Volunteer Instructor
**O'Dea High School | August 2024 – 2025 (Program's Final Year)**

In August 2024, I joined the Microsoft TEALS (Technology Education and Literacy in Schools) program, which pairs tech industry volunteers with high school classrooms to teach computer science. I taught Python fundamentals to seniors at O'Dea High School for one full school year, adapting the TEALS national curriculum for my classroom and building out example projects and assignment boilerplate on GitHub Education — all in Python — so O'Dea could keep running the course smoothly after I left.

This one was a full-circle moment for me — my own interest in computer science started in a TEALS classroom back in 2017, when I was on the other side of the desk as a high school student. As it turned out, my year teaching would be the program's last; Microsoft decommissioned TEALS after that school year, so I'm glad I got the chance to be part of it before it wrapped up. Getting to pay that forward with my own class was one of the more meaningful things I've done.

## Guest Lecturer – AI & Industry Topics
**North Seattle College | Washington State University**

I gave my first guest lecture on AI and its impact on the tech industry at North Seattle College in February 2025, and returned to my alma mater, WSU, starting in October 2025 for two to three guest lectures — with an ongoing goal of making it back annually:

- **AI and industry trends:** I spoke to freshman-level classes about how the tech industry is scaling with AI, using GitHub's Spec Kit as a case study just two weeks after its release to walk students through how these tools work in practice.
- **Industry perspective:** I've guest lectured in upper-level (300/400) courses on what it's actually like working in the industry, giving students a grounded look at day-to-day engineering work beyond the classroom.
- **WSU Software Engineering Club:** In spring 2026, I was invited to speak with the club directly about my experience working in industry.

## Claude Desktop Training & AI Enablement Docs
**Expedia Group**

I designed and led a training session on Claude Desktop for roughly 800 people across Expedia, primarily aimed at a non-technical audience — PMs, finance, and other non-engineering roles. The goal was to teach practical fundamentals people could apply immediately to improve their day-to-day workflows. While the live session was a one-time event so far, I've stayed closely involved with the Claude team at Expedia and written extensive internal documentation on onboarding to AI tools — docs that have since reached a few thousand unique visitors.

## Founder, Expedia Finance Club

Outside of technical teaching, I founded the Expedia Finance Club as a bit of a passion project — a community focused on personal finance education, covering the basics like IRAs, investment account types, and core financial literacy topics I've always cared about. The club has grown to roughly 300 members since I started it. It's had one flagship event so far, and I'm looking to run more, though balancing it alongside my day-to-day work is an ongoing challenge.
